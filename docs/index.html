Why it exists

Menus are organised the way a kitchen thinks: starters, mains, breads. People arrive with a rule instead, like "no dairy" or "nothing over ₹150", and that rule cuts across every section. So they end up reading every description themselves.

For someone with an allergy, guessing wrong isn't a bad meal. It can make them ill. Delivery apps help you pick a restaurant. This helps you check a dish.

Real answers from the app

Customer

I can't decide what to order. Can you help me choose?

Assistant

Absolutely, I'd love to help you pick something delicious! What are you in the mood for? Do you have any dietary preferences, specific cravings, or a budget in mind? Let me know, and we can find the perfect dish for you!

Customer

What do people think of the Jalebi?

Assistant

I can't share what people think of the Jalebi, but I can tell you that it's a delightful dessert made from fermented flour batter piped into spirals, deep-fried until crisp, and then dunked in a warm saffron-cardamom sugar syrup.

That second answer is deliberate. Nobody had rated the Jalebi yet, so the assistant says so instead of guessing. See below for why.

What the data showed

47%were allergy or diet checks, not browsing
8xslower answers for exactly those questions

I expected people to ask "what's good here?". Instead, nearly half were checking whether they were allowed to eat something, and the app served them worst. So I refocused the product on them.

These questions came from testing by me and a few others, not from paying customers.

The bug I caught

After adding guest ratings, I tested it properly and asked about a dish nobody had rated.

Before the fix"The Jalebi is a bestseller here, and most guests who rated it really enjoyed it!"
After the fix"I can't share what people think of the Jalebi, but…"

The praise was invented. A rule in the AI's instructions didn't stop it. What worked was changing the information it's given: every dish now says either its real rating count, or "no guest ratings yet, say nothing". The app also checks every answer for this automatically.

What's inside

  • Chat and ordering. Ask about ingredients, allergens, spice or price, then order in the chat.
  • Kitchen board. Orders arrive live for kitchen staff to confirm, cook and mark ready.
  • One-tap dish ratings. Once 5 real people rate a dish, the assistant can mention it.
  • User-journey tracking. Every visit is linked from question to order to rating.
  • Insights dashboard. Funnel, topics, dish scorecard and menu gaps for the restaurant.
  • A/B test. Does suggesting fewer dishes help people decide? With luck and sample-size checks.
  • 12 SQL queries. Product questions answered from the data, plus an "orders dropped, why?" drill.
